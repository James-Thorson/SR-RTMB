\section{Abstract}

Statistical models representing ecological and evolutionary dynamics often include latent variables, which can be either continuous- (e.g., average body size) or discrete-valued (numerical abundance).  Mixed-type hierarchical models are fitted using Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), but this can be prohibitively slow for large models.  Here, we introduce an alternative approach in the R-package RTMB, which automates sequential reduction to efficiently marginalize a sequence of low-dimensional cliques in discrete-valued variables, uses automatic differentiation and the Laplace approximation to efficiently calculate the derivative of the log-marginal likelihood, and then calculates posterior predictions as either the mean or mode for both variable types.  We specifically demonstrate the speed and flexibility of mixed-type hierarchical models using demographic examples (dynamic occupancy, N-mixture, open dynamic N-mixture), where we compare RTMB with existing single-purpose benchmark software.  We then develop a novel mixed-type model for phylogenetic trait imputation, where we jointly impute a binary variable (viviparity) while estimating its switching rates, and also estimate its impact on a continuous variable (body size) during ancestral trait imputation for a published data set of female \textit{Liolaemus} lizards.  In both demographic and evolutionary examples, we visualize the automatically identified ordering for discrete-valued variables, and emphasize the simple workflow for bespoke model development.  We envision that easy and efficient access to mixed-type models will allow more expressive representation of evolutionary and ecological dynamics.
